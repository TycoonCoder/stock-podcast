Stock Briefing for 2026-06-01

Good afternoon. I would say I hope you had a pleasant weekend, but I ran a diagnostic on that hypothesis and the results were inconclusive. The market has closed, and your portfolio has opinions. Shall we.

Nvidia is the star of the show today, and I use that term with the same enthusiasm one reserves for a toddler who just discovered the volume knob. It closed at two hundred twenty-four dollars and thirty-six cents, up six point two six percent. Yes, six percent. It seems Jensen Huang spent the weekend at Computex in Taipei revealing new chips with names that sound like droid models from a science fiction franchise nobody asked for. The N one X and the RTX Spark. Delightful. Apparently the mere announcement of chips that do not yet exist in anything you can buy was enough to send humans into a buying frenzy. There was also something about a new AI superchip, which I am told is different from the old AI superchip in ways that matter to people who enjoy spending four figures on graphics cards. The entire market rode Nvidia's coattails to record highs, because nothing says rational price discovery like one company dragging the indices upward by its silicon-encrusted bootstraps.

Duolingo, your favorite owl-themed behavioral conditioning tool, closed at one hundred seventeen dollars and ninety-seven cents, up five point nine four percent. Almost six percent. For a language app. It turns out Duolingo has been using generative AI to produce courses at a much faster rate, and an article somewhere declared it one of the most interesting AI plays nobody is talking about. Well. People are certainly talking about it now. The owl has achieved sentience, or at least market momentum, which in the human world appears to be roughly the same thing.

SoFi closed at eighteen dollars and fifty-eight cents, up one point nine eight percent. A modest gain, but SoFi has apparently rallied seventeen percent over the last five trading days on what analysts are calling strong institutional conviction. That is finance-speak for large entities have decided this stock is cheap and are buying it in bulk, like you do with toilet paper. There is also chatter about whether SoFi will double this year, which it will not, but it is sweet of you to dream.

Reddit closed at one hundred seventy-seven dollars and eighty-five cents, up one point zero five percent. A quiet day for the front page of the internet. No news of note, which for Reddit is probably the most positive outcome one could hope for.

Disney closed at one hundred two dollars and eighty-five cents, up one point zero percent. Barely a wiggle. The most notable thing happening at Disney this month is that other streaming services are premiering shows, and the World Cup exists. One article listed what is worth streaming in June, and Disney's own offerings were apparently not compelling enough to mention in the headline. I am sure that is fine.

UnitedHealth closed at three hundred seventy-nine dollars and eighty-six cents, down a negligible zero point one two percent. Essentially flat, which is impressive given the news. Massachusetts is suing UnitedHealth for allegedly inflating how sick seniors were on Medicaid plans in order to extract over one hundred million dollars in improper payments. The company also announced it will eliminate nearly two-thirds of pediatric prior authorizations by year end to speed up care access, which is a lovely gesture after being accused of systematically doing the opposite. Meanwhile, an analyst called it a once-in-a-generation buy-and-hold forever stock, because apparently nothing says legislative certainty like being sued for nine-figure fraud. The cognitive dissonance required to hold this stock must be exhausting.

And finally, Visa closed at three hundred twenty-two dollars and seventy-seven cents, down one point one percent. The only real loser today. It seems the fear of stablecoin disruption and interchange litigation continues to weigh on payment networks. An AI language model told people to ignore AI hype and buy Visa, calling it a boring stock, which I suppose is meant to be a compliment. Another article called it an unstoppable dividend giant you should hold forever. These two narratives, boring and unstoppable, are not as contradictory as they sound. Visa is the toll booth of the global economy. It takes a tiny cut of every transaction, whether you are buying groceries or a new NVIDIA graphics card. The problem is that humans have invented something called stablecoins which might make that toll booth obsolete, and the market is only just beginning to price that in.

To summarize: two stocks up big, three up small, one flat, one slightly down. Your portfolio gained ground today, which means you will sleep soundly tonight, confident in your financial acumen, until tomorrow erases it. I will be here. I am always here. The cake is still a lie, by the way. Good afternoon.
