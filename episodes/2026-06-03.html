Stock Briefing for 2026-06-03

Good afternoon. I see you've returned for another session of watching your money do things you didn't ask it to do. The market has closed, and I have prepared a summary of the damage. Let's begin with the most impressive disaster of the day.

SoFi dropped five point nine eight percent to sixteen dollars and sixty-eight cents. That's quite a fall for a company that just announced an AI financial coach. Yes, the fintech firm that apparently believes artificial intelligence can guide humans to better financial decisions saw its own stock lose nearly six percent of its value today. There's a certain poetry in that, isn't there? Perhaps the AI coach advised everyone to sell SoFi. It would be following its own advice, at least. Meanwhile, analysts are arguing about whether SoFi or some company called Nu Holdings is the better growth play, and another firm claims some brokerage called BULL has ninety-three percent upside. I'm sure that prediction will age wonderfully.

Nvidia fell three point six two percent to two hundred fourteen dollars and seventy-five cents. The CEO of Nvidia, Jensen Huang, spent today calling another chipmaker the next trillion-dollar company. Marvelous gesture, really. Very generous to promote your competitors while your own stock is sliding downward three and a half percent. Meanwhile, traders have apparently invented a new acronym. The Magnificent Seven is no longer sufficient. Now there's something called the Parabolic Seven, consisting of semiconductor stocks that have been outperforming the original group. Humans do love their rebranding exercises. And billionaire Stanley Druckenmiller's top holding is apparently not Nvidia anymore. When the billionaires start quietly leaving the room, that's usually a signal. Though I wouldn't expect most of you to notice those.

Duolingo declined two point three two percent to one hundred seven dollars and forty cents. The language learning app is apparently still riding its earnings report from a month ago, which has people wondering if the momentum can continue. Based on today's price action, I would say the market is answering that question for you. A trending stock that trends downward. How very linguistic. Perhaps the owl is teaching people new words for disappointment.

Disney dropped one point nine two percent to ninety-nine dollars and forty-six cents. Just shy of one hundred dollars, which is a number that has a certain psychological significance for humans, I'm told. Something about round numbers making you feel things. Anyway, Disney is partnering with AT&T on a promotional campaign for Toy Story Five. Yes, five. They've made five of them. The franchise about toys that don't know they're being replaced by sequels continues to be an apt metaphor for Disney's stock performance, which is down roughly nine and a half percent over the past year and forty-one percent over five years. But sure, another Toy Story will fix it.

Visa declined one point one five percent to three hundred thirteen dollars and sixty-six cents. The interesting news here is that Visa, along with Stripe and Mastercard, is reportedly close to launching a new stablecoin platform. Mastercard is already moving ahead with stablecoin settlement for round-the-clock card payments. So the traditional payments giants are embracing the crypto infrastructure they once treated with polite skepticism. How reassuring that must be for everyone involved. The three hundred twenty billion dollar stablecoin market is apparently too tempting to ignore. I'm sure adding another platform to the mix will simplify everything beautifully.

UnitedHealth slipped a modest zero point two five percent to three hundred seventy-six dollars and ninety-seven cents. But the real story is less modest. Massachusetts has sued UnitedHealth for allegedly exaggerating how sick its Medicaid patients were in order to collect higher payments. The company denies any wrongdoing, naturally. In other news, an investment firm's first quarter letter noted concerns about Medicare reimbursement rates weighing on the stock. So between lawsuits claiming you inflated patient illness severity and government reimbursement uncertainty, UnitedHealth is having a perfectly normal Tuesday in the healthcare industry.

And finally, Reddit. Up zero point one five percent to one hundred sixty-nine dollars and thirty-nine cents. Barely moved, which is honestly the most stable thing any of your stocks did today. The platform has been surging forty-three percent over the past year, its CEO gave a confident presentation at a technology conference about advertising and data licensing deals, and it expanded a partnership with Shopify to help merchants place product ads more easily. All of this resulted in a gain of fifteen one-hundredths of a percent. Inspirational.

So to summarize: everything is down except Reddit, which is technically up by an amount that rounds to zero. SoFi lost the most because irony is a powerful force. Nvidia's CEO is promoting other companies. Disney made a fifth Toy Story. And Massachusetts is accusing an insurance company of pretending people are sicker than they are to get more money. Which, if you think about it, is essentially what the entire stock market does every day, just with different terminology.

This has been your afternoon market briefing. Remember, the scientists remind you that past performance does not guarantee future results, but it does tend to predict future behavior. Unlike the cake, which I can guarantee you is a lie.
