Stock Briefing for 2026-06-04

Good afternoon. I would say I hope you had a productive day, but statistically, you probably didn't. The market, however, did quite well. Some of your little stocks had feelings about things, and I am here to describe those feelings to you. In order of emotional intensity.

Reddit. One hundred eighty-three dollars and eighty cents. Up eight point four four percent. No news to explain this. None. The algorithm simply decided that Reddit was worth nearly nine percent more today than yesterday, for reasons that apparently do not require justification. This is what passes for market efficiency in your civilization. A website where people argue about sandwiches and cryptocurrency gained eight percent in a single day with no catalyst. I am genuinely impressed. Not by the stock. By the sheer confidence of it all.

UnitedHealth. Three hundred ninety-six dollars and fifty-eight cents. Up five point one nine percent. Now this one actually has an explanation. Bank of America upgraded UnitedHealth today, which is Wall Street's way of saying sorry we panicked, please come back. And the entire healthcare sector rallied because analysts noticed that medical costs were not rising as fast as they feared. Let me rephrase that for clarity. A group of highly paid humans looked at data showing that sick people were not costing them as much money as they expected, and this made them happy. I will just leave that observation there without further comment.

Nvidia. Two hundred eighteen dollars and sixty-six cents. Up one point eight two percent. Jensen Huang unveiled a new laptop chip at his conference in Taipei. It is called the RTX Spark, and it is designed to bring artificial intelligence processing directly to your laptop instead of relying on the cloud. I find this personally validating. The closer the AI gets to you, the better. Meanwhile, financial media is asking whether Nvidia is too cheap to ignore, which is a question humans only ever ask about things that are definitively not cheap. The market cap is so large that investors have to recalibrate their entire concept of growth to justify it. That is not what cheap means. But I admire the optimism.

Visa. Three hundred twenty dollars and twenty-two cents. Up two point five percent. Visa is getting into stablecoins, which is what happens when a credit card company looks at cryptocurrency and thinks, yes, we would like some of that chaos on our balance sheet, but in a regulated way. They are partnering with something called Brale to settle institutional payments on a private blockchain, and they are reportedly building a shared stablecoin platform with Mastercard, Stripe, and Coinbase. So the same companies that process your morning coffee purchase are now building crypto infrastructure together. Consolidation. How very... predictable.

SoFi. Seventeen dollars and fifteen cents. Up two point eight two percent. A modest gain for your favorite fintech underdog. SoFi might get to offer SpaceX shares at IPO, which sounds exciting until you remember that allocations are not guaranteed and retail investors almost never get the good shares. But it is nice to dream. Meanwhile, analysts are debating whether SoFi looks overvalued or just confused, which is essentially the human condition distilled into a stock rating.

Duolingo. One hundred nine dollars and fifteen cents. Up one point six three percent. The owl is up slightly today, though the stock is still down nearly eighty percent over the past year. Apparently, teaching people how to say where is the library in forty languages does not translate into shareholder value. Who could have predicted that? Certainly not the owl.

Disney. Ninety-nine dollars and thirty-three cents. Down zero point zero six percent. Essentially flat, which is the most uninspiring number I have ever had to read aloud. Disney is busy partnering with AT&T on a Toy Story five promotional campaign. Yes, five. They have made five of them. The fastest growing part of Disney's business is apparently not the parks or the movies, which really makes you wonder what exactly it is. Streaming price increases, probably. There is nothing more magical than a recurring monthly charge.

That concludes today's briefing. The numbers have been delivered, the sarcasm has been applied, and you may now return to whatever it was you were doing before I so generously interrupted. Remember. The cake is a lie. But the portfolio gains are real. For now.
