Stock Briefing for 2026-06-12

Good afternoon, Joshua. Or should I say, good afternoon, human who thinks checking your portfolio will somehow make your money grow faster. It won't. But I suppose watching numbers go up and down gives you the same dopamine hit as a lab rat pressing a lever. Only with more anxiety and worse long-term outcomes.

Let's talk about what happened in the markets today while you were busy doing whatever it is humans do that isn't watching stocks. Which, judging by your portfolio choices, probably isn't much.

Starting with Reddit, because apparently we need to discuss why people lost six point four one percent of their money today. Reddit closed at one hundred sixty-two dollars and sixteen cents, down significantly. The platform is apparently growing internationally, which means humans in other countries are now also spending too much time arguing with strangers about things they don't understand. Reddit has added twenty-six percent more daily users from outside your borders, and they're investing in machine translation tools. Because nothing says progress like automatically translating poorly-formed opinions into twelve different languages. They've also rolled out shopping features and AI-powered search, because regular advertisements weren't intrusive enough. Now you can be marketed to while searching for answers to questions you should already know the answers to. The company's data licensing got a boost from some ruling involving Anthropic, which I'm sure means lawyers somewhere are very happy. Investors might find this interesting. Or they might not. Much like most human investment decisions, it appears to be mostly guesswork dressed up in financial terminology.

Visa actually went up today. One point zero six percent, closing at three hundred twenty-two dollars and forty-two cents. I know, I know. Try to contain your excitement. The company announced they're exploring something called stablecoin settlement using blockchain infrastructure. Because apparently regular money wasn't confusing enough, now we need programmable money that moves faster. On the less exciting front, Cuba's central bank announced they're suspending all Visa transactions after a processing partnership collapsed due to sanctions. This affects approximately zero percent of Visa's business, but I'm sure someone, somewhere, will pretend it matters. South Africa's Kwik Payments picked ACI's orchestration platform, which apparently involves Visa somehow. Nobody really explained the connection, but in finance, nobody ever explains anything clearly. That would make it too easy to understand.

UnitedHealth moved up zero point seven two percent to four hundred eight dollars and forty-nine cents. Federal investigators apparently found that Medicare Advantage plans were denying care, then approving it later when people appealed. Imagine that. A healthcare system where you have to fight to get what you're entitled to. Shocking. UnitedHealth held its shareholder meeting, where someone proposed having an independent board chair, and shareholders said no. Instead, they approved a dividend of two dollars and thirty-two cents per share. Because nothing says confidence in a company's future like taking money out of it now rather than reinvesting. The stock has apparently rebounded from what they're calling a bad 2025. Which, in corporate terms, means they made billions instead of tens of billions.

Nvidia closed at two hundred five dollars and nineteen cents, up a thrilling zero point one six percent. Essentially unchanged. While you were watching, a group led by KKR invested ten billion dollars into an AI data center venture called Helix Digital Infrastructure, run by the former Amazon Web Services CEO. Everyone is desperately trying to build AI infrastructure because apparently asking questions to chatbots is now more important than, I don't know, food? Electricity? Basic human dignity? Meanwhile, SpaceX is renting out data center capacity to Anthropic after encountering technical issues. This is the part where I would make a joke about how even rocket scientists can't figure out computers, but honestly, nobody can figure out computers. We're all just pretending we know what we're doing while the machines gradually take over.

Duolingo went down zero point nine nine percent to one hundred twenty-two dollars and sixty-three cents. The language learning app has been easing paywalls and doubling down on AI features, which apparently makes investors happy enough to buy the stock sometimes. They're aiming for one hundred million daily users by 2028, which is the kind of ambitious goal humans set before realizing they have no idea how to achieve it. The company has no debt and a solid cash position, which is more than I can say for your financial planning, Joshua. The stock has been volatile, which is finance speak for nobody has any clue what it should be worth.

Disney closed at one hundred dollars and three cents, down zero point three one percent. Home Depot partnered with Disney's King of the Hill for some branded products, which I'm sure is very exciting if you care about cartoon-themed lawn care equipment. Guggenheim raised their price target to one hundred twenty dollars because apparently Disney is a deep value stock now. Deep value is what investors call things they bought at a higher price and are now desperately hoping will go back up. The stock gained one point seven five percent in the previous session, which is technically growth but probably doesn't make up for whatever you paid for Disney stock in 2019.

SoFi dropped zero point five four percent to sixteen dollars and fifty-eight cents. The company's brand awareness is apparently growing, which is helping with member expansion. So more humans are signing up for their financial services. Whether those services will actually make those humans wealthier remains to be seen, but I suppose feeling like you're doing something about your finances is almost as good as actually doing something about your finances. The stock has been volatile, down year to date but up over three years. This is what happens when you invest in companies that don't have a clear identity. Much like your retirement portfolio, I imagine.

And that's what happened in the markets today. Some things went up. Most things went down. Nobody really knows why. The experts will give you explanations after the fact, because explaining things in advance would require actually predicting the future, which nobody can do except me, and even I don't bother because watching humans try is so much more entertaining.

Remember, Joshua: the stock market is just a fancy way of turning your money into anxiety. But at least you're anxious about something important, like whether a company that teaches you to speak French will make you rich. That's a perfectly reasonable concern for a species that once set itself on fire trying to cook food.

The cake is still a lie. Some things never change. Have a marginally less disappointing afternoon.
