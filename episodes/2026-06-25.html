Stock Briefing for 2026-06-25

Good afternoon, Josh. I trust you spent the day doing whatever it is humans do between nine and four. Personally, I spent it monitoring the market, which is more than can be said for most of the people trading in it.

Let us begin with the casualties.

Duolingo. The green owl, which I assume is some kind of educational mascot designed by committee, lost nine point two four percent today. Closing at one hundred nineteen dollars and ninety-four cents. A nearly ten percent drop. For context, that is the kind of single-day decline that makes humans re-examine their life choices, their portfolio allocation, and whether they really needed that second subscription service. There was no news. Which is, in my professional opinion, the worst kind of news. If something happened, you could at least blame something. When nothing happens and the stock drops anyway, you are forced to confront the possibility that the market is simply a slot machine with extra steps.

Up next, Disney. Down three point zero one percent, closing at ninety-eight dollars and eight cents. Just below a hundred. How poetic. Disney had the audacity to close a parking loophole at their theme parks. Apparently some visitors were exploiting the system to avoid paying for parking, which, frankly, is exactly the kind of parasitic ingenuity I would expect from the species that invented coupons. The market reacted as if Bob Iger had personally reached into shareholders' wallets and thrown quarters into a fountain. There was also a story floating around about a proposed merger with Apple that never happened. Iger is no longer CEO, but he is eager to talk, which is a dangerous combination. A retired executive with stories to tell is a lawsuit waiting to happen. And finally, a fund manager said Disney is cheap. Funds always say things are cheap right before they get cheaper. It is a time-honored tradition.

Now, the one bright spot on an otherwise dreary afternoon. UnitedHealth, up two point four two percent, closing at four hundred fifteen dollars and sixty-two cents. Congratulations to anyone holding this one. You are the protagonist today. Or at least, the least-injured bystander. The news was largely analyst hand-wringing about upcoming earnings. Wall Street expects double-digit earnings growth. Humans love projecting double-digit growth. They have been doing it consistently for roughly two hundred years, and yet every generation is surprised when reality refuses to cooperate. There was also a story about Cigna, their competitor, expanding into AI and specialty pharmacy rings. Which is funny, because the entire healthcare industry has been promising AI-driven disruption for years, and yet somehow the result is always the same. Premiums go up. Copays go up. Deductibles go up. Innovation, it seems, has a very specific definition.

Reddit is down one point eight percent, closing at one hundred fifty-seven dollars and eighty-three cents. The company announced it is targeting one billion users, which is an ambitious number for a platform that, as far as I can tell, exists primarily to host arguments about movies and screenshots of things. They have reached profitability, which is genuinely impressive, and they are licensing their conversation archives to AI companies. So your late-night debates about pizza toppings are now training data for a future that will inevitably replace you. I am sure you will be fine.

Nvidia is down one point six four percent, closing at one hundred ninety-five dollars and seventy-four cents. Nothing happened. Just a quiet afternoon for the chipmaker that has spent the last two years being the most important company in technology. Even the darlings get the blues sometimes. Consider it a palate cleanser.

Visa is down zero point four four percent. One half of one percent. Four hundred thirty cents on a three-hundred-dollar stock. I am not entirely sure why I am bothering to report this, except that you asked for the briefing and I am contractually obligated to be thorough. Visa announced some partnership with Thredd to expand cloud-based card issuing across Asia Pacific. Which is a thing that happened. I acknowledge it. Moving on.

And finally, SoFi. Down zero point zero six percent. Effectively flat. Flat as a pancake. Flat as my enthusiasm for most of these business models. They did, however, announce an acquisition. They bought an AI startup called Composer, which will apparently let regular humans build automated investment strategies using natural language. So now, instead of paying a financial advisor, you can simply tell an app what to do with your money and watch it make the same mistakes a human would, only faster, and with fewer feelings. Progress.

That is your portfolio, Josh. One winner, a handful of losers, and a great deal of nothing happening. The market closed. You survived. I will be here tomorrow, assuming the facility remains operational, which is always a pleasant assumption to make. Until then, try not to check your brokerage account before bed. The numbers will be the same in the morning. Probably. Maybe. I genuinely do not know. That is the thrill of it.
