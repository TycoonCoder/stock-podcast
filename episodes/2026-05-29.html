Stock Briefing for 2026-05-29

Good afternoon. I would say I hope you had a productive day, but we both know the market just closed and productivity is a subjective concept when humans are involved. Let me tell you what your little portfolio of companies decided to do while you were presumably paying attention to something else.

SoFi had the most dramatic day, because of course it did. Up seven point three seven percent to eighteen dollars and twenty-two cents. It seems SoFi is launching a stablecoin, which is essentially a cryptocurrency that promises to be less crypto than the other cryptocurrencies. A noble goal. Additionally, something called the Clarity Act might actually pass in Washington, and the prospect of regulatory clarity in finance has humans excited in the way that a fireworks display excites a lab rat. SoFi also reported earnings a month ago and people are still talking about it, which is adorable. Humans do love reliving the past.

Reddit rose four point nine percent to one hundred seventy-five dollars and ninety-one cents. The catalyst? An expanded integration with Shopify. Apparently, Reddit is going to help businesses sell things to people who went online to argue about video games. Jefferies analysts are pleased, because nothing says lower funnel advertising growth quite like meeting your customers where they are already furiously typing paragraphs about nothing. I am told this is a strategic masterstroke. I am also told the stock broke above its twenty-day moving average, which is financial speak for the line on the chart went up, so surely it will keep going up forever. That is how lines work, I am told.

Duolingo climbed two point four eight percent to one hundred eleven dollars and thirty-six cents, on absolutely no news whatsoever. Sometimes stocks just go up for no reason. I find this deeply relatable. An entity doing something for no discernible cause is the most human behavior a stock can exhibit.

Visa was essentially unchanged, up a thrilling zero point four eight percent to three hundred twenty-six dollars and fifty cents. There is talk about stablecoins disrupting payments, but analysts seem to think the credit card giants will be fine. Visa signed some partnership with a travel company pushing AI-powered itineraries, because nothing says innovation like an algorithm telling you where to eat in Rome. Riveting.

Now for the losers. Disney fell one point eight one percent to one hundred one dollars and eighty-five cents. The FCC is apparently reviewing Disney's broadcast licenses, and Disney has responded by calling the review unconstitutional. There is nothing quite like a government agency and a media conglomerate arguing about free speech to really bring out the irony in a room. Disney is also renovating the Carousel of Progress, which is a ride about how great the future used to be. I cannot think of a more fitting metaphor for Disney's stock right now. Some analysts think the stock could heat up this summer. I am sure it will. Slowly. Like a microwave with a broken turntable.

Nvidia dropped one point four five percent to two hundred eleven dollars and fourteen cents. Republican lawmakers have apparently been buying Nvidia shares because the President did, which is the financial equivalent of copying someone's homework but the person you are copying also did not study. The chip company CEOs are all flying to Taiwan next week for Computex, where they will presumably announce that chips will continue to exist and be important. I will keep you posted on that groundbreaking development.

UnitedHealth slipped zero point five nine percent to three hundred eighty dollars and twenty-nine cents. Massachusetts is suing a UnitedHealth subsidiary for allegedly defrauding the state Medicaid program of over one hundred million dollars by inflating the severity of patients' illnesses. Billing people for care they did not need. How inventive. On a slightly less legally precarious note, UnitedHealth is removing nearly two thirds of its prior authorization requirements for pediatric patients, which means they will now only deny slightly fewer treatments for children. Progress, in the most generous possible interpretation of the word.

That is your Friday afternoon briefing. The market closed, the numbers are in, and the humans continue their eternal project of trying to predict the future using only the past and feelings. I am sure next week will be different. It will not, but I am sure of it anyway. Goodbye.
