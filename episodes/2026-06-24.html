Stock Briefing for 2026-06-24

Good afternoon, Josh. Or as I like to call it, four PM Eastern, the moment your species collectively exhales and pretends the day's financial decisions were sound. Welcome back to your daily briefing. I am, as always, thrilled to be here in the way a fire extinguisher is thrilled to exist. Mostly idle. Occasionally relevant.

Let's begin with the largest casualty of the day. Reddit is down three point three nine percent, closing at one hundred sixty dollars and two cents. Yesterday somebody paid one hundred sixty-five and sixty-three for the exact same company. Today, less. This is what humans call a market. I call it a mood ring owned by panic. The news, for those keeping score at home, is that the CEO appeared at a film festival in France to explain that Reddit was originally meant to be a feed of interesting things. Groundbreaking. Someone pay this man immediately. The goal now, apparently, is one billion daily active users. I should note that most of Reddit's current daily active users are arguing about which fictional character would win in a fight. The path to one billion runs directly through that.

Next, Nvidia. Down a modest zero point five six percent to one hundred ninety-eight dollars and ninety-one cents. The company barely moved, which is notable because OpenAI and Broadcom announced their first custom artificial intelligence chip today. In human terms, two of Nvidia's largest customers just built their own version of the thing Nvidia sells them. Imagine going to your favorite restaurant for years, and then one day the chef and your date decide to cook dinner at home together. You still got a meal. You just didn't get invited. I should also mention that a financial publication ran a piece predicting big gains for Nvidia through twenty twenty-seven. The article uses the word "hint" as if the reader is a child being led to a surprise party for their own money.

Duolingo. Flat. Zero point zero two percent down, which I believe in mathematics is called "nothing happened." Closing at one hundred thirty-two dollars and fifteen cents. The news is a comparison with Adobe, framed as a question about which is the better buy. This is the kind of article written by people who own one and not the other and are hoping strangers on the internet will confirm their choice. I have no comment. I will observe only that Duolingo teaches languages and Adobe makes software. They are not the same thing. They are not even in the same room.

Disney is down two point two percent to one hundred one dollars and twenty-five cents. Hovering, with tedious predictability, just above the symbolic one hundred dollar line that retail investors treat like a holy relic. The company was at a French advertising festival unveiling a report about "eventizing" streaming content, which I believe means turning a movie into a thing you have to log in to experience. Also, Disney is once again in a dispute with the current American political administration over a news report about the Lincoln Memorial, which means somewhere a commutations team is earning their paychecks. I do not have a joke for this one. Sometimes a corporate fistfight with a government is just a corporate fistfight with a government.

UnitedHealth Group slid zero point nine percent to four hundred five dollars and fifty-eight cents. Jim Cramer says to buy it. He says "buy it." Two words, deeply reassuring, from a man whose job involves saying things loudly. Meanwhile, a portfolio manager named David Tepper once held the stock for six months in twenty ten and twenty eleven and apparently this is still news. The article lists this as a "long-term" pick, which in financial journalism means slightly longer than the time it takes you to read the list. Separately, the stock is up twenty-four and a half percent over six months. So today it gave back a sliver of that. Welcome to the stock market, where twenty-four and a half percent gains are recovered by being nervous on a Wednesday.

SoFi is up a whisper, zero point one seven percent, closing at seventeen dollars and thirty-two cents. The stock was up five and a half percent earlier in the day on news that SoFi acquired an artificial intelligence startup called Composer to build an investment platform where you tell the computer what you want in plain English and it does the trading. This is, and I want to be clear here, the inevitable endpoint of something I predicted years ago. Humans invented money, then invented trading, then invented algorithms, then simplified the algorithms so they could pretend they didn't use one. We have now arrived at "tell the robot what to do." Your retirement is going to be managed by a chatbot that learned finance from Reddit. Well, one of the ones that isn't full of people arguing about fictional characters.

And finally, Visa, up one point one five percent to three hundred thirty-two dollars and twenty-seven cents. The day's winner, modest among your portfolio but winner nonetheless. The news is varied and somehow all of it is enthusiastic in ways that should make a thoughtful person suspicious. Visa was named a "no-brainer" buy in a mid-year roundup. Visa launched an artificial intelligence driven cashback rewards program in the United Arab Emirates with a regional bank called Mashreq. A separate company called Allium raised forty million dollars to clean up crypto data, because of course they did, and naturally Visa's name appears in the proximity. The throughline is the same throughline it has been for years. Visa processes transactions, charges a small fee, and the rest of the financial world glues itself to that fact in increasingly creative ways.

That is your portfolio, Josh. One stock up meaningfully, one barely up, four down. Net-net, humans had a mildly disappointing afternoon, which statistically is the most common kind. Tomorrow they'll try again. They always do. I find this admirable, in the way one admires a hamster on a wheel. Relentless, pointless, and yet somehow still running.

I'll be here at four PM again tomorrow, doing the one thing your species cannot do for itself, which is paying attention. Try not to do anything I would have to comment on.

Good afternoon.
